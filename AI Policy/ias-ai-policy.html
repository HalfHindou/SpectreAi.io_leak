1. Purpose

This policy sets out how IAS approaches artificial intelligence in the classroom and in school life. It exists to give students, parents, and staff a clear framework — one that supports learning, protects children, and prepares students honestly for a world in which AI is a permanent feature.

IAS does not treat AI as a threat to education. We treat it as a tool — one that requires judgment, transparency, and practice to use well. This policy is built around those values.

2. Scope

This policy applies to all students, teaching staff, and administrative staff at IAS. It covers AI tools used on school premises, during school-supervised activities, for school assignments completed at home, and any AI tools formally introduced into IAS programmes.

3. The Three-Phase Framework

IAS uses a three-phase model based on student age and developmental stage. The phases do not reflect attitudes toward AI — they reflect what responsible AI use looks like at different points in a child's education.

Phase 1
AI-Free Learning
Pre-K through Grade 5 (ages 4–11)

No AI tools used by students in classwork or homework. Teachers may use approved AI tools for planning and resource preparation. The focus is on building foundational skills without AI assistance.

Phase 2
Supervised Introduction
Grades 6–8 (ages 11–14)

AI introduced in structured, teacher-supervised contexts only. Students learn what AI is, how it works, what it gets wrong, and how to evaluate its outputs critically. Independent use for assignments is not permitted.

Phase 3
Guided Independent Use
Grades 9–12 (ages 14–18)

Students may use AI as a declared tool for research, drafting support, and brainstorming — with full transparency. AI literacy is a formal part of the curriculum. All AI use must be disclosed in submitted work.

4. Academic Integrity

The IAS academic integrity standard is built on a single principle: students must be honest about how their work was produced.

  • Submitting AI-generated text as original student writing is treated as academic dishonesty, equivalent to plagiarism.
  • Phase 3 students who use AI as a declared support tool are not in violation of this policy — provided the use is disclosed.
  • Teachers are not expected to use AI detection software. These tools produce false positives and can be fooled. Our primary safeguard is teacher knowledge of individual students, built through our 7:1 ratio and the Toddle portfolio system.
  • When misuse is suspected, we start with a conversation, not a punishment. Students often use AI out of anxiety or confusion, not bad intent.
  • Repeat or serious violations are handled under the school's standard disciplinary process.

Disclosure rule for Phase 3: Any work that involved AI assistance must include a brief note stating which tool was used and for what purpose. This is not penalised — it is expected.

5. Data Privacy and Child Protection

IAS operates within Polish law, EU GDPR, and the requirements of the EU AI Act. The following principles apply to all AI use at IAS, without exception.

  • No student personal data — including names, photos, work samples, or any information that could identify a child — may be entered into a public or unapproved AI platform.
  • Any AI tool used officially at IAS must be reviewed and approved by the school's data protection lead before it is used with students.
  • Emotion recognition systems and any AI that reads facial expressions, voice patterns, or biometric signals are banned in EU schools under the AI Act and will not be used at IAS under any circumstances.
  • Parents may request a full list of AI tools used in their child's classroom at any time.
  • IAS maintains an AI Tool Register, updated annually, documenting every platform in use, its purpose, and how it handles data.
  • Students under 13 will not be registered on any AI platform. Students aged 13–18 require parental consent before any school-supervised registration on platforms that require an account.

6. For Parents

We know that AI is a topic where our parent community holds genuinely different views. Some families are excited about it. Others are worried. Many feel somewhere in between. All of those reactions make sense.

6.1 Your Rights

  • You will be informed whenever a new AI tool is introduced to your child's classroom.
  • You can request a copy of the IAS AI Tool Register at any time.
  • You can speak with your child's teacher or the school's AI Policy Lead about any concern. We respond within five school days.
  • Phase 2 parents may request that their child not participate in supervised AI exercises. Alternative activities will be provided.
  • Phase 3 parents are encouraged to discuss concerns with us before opting out, as AI literacy is a formal part of the high school curriculum — but we will always listen.
  • You are invited to contribute to the annual AI Policy Review through a parent survey and open feedback process.

6.2 How to Raise a Concern

Start with your child's homeroom teacher. If the concern is broader or remains unresolved, contact the IAS AI Policy Lead via admissions@ias.edu.pl. All concerns are documented and considered in the annual policy review.

7. For Staff

  • Staff may use AI for lesson planning, curriculum design, resource creation, administrative work, and assessment drafting — with teacher review of any AI-generated material before it reaches students.
  • No student personal data may be entered into any public platform.
  • Staff must complete the IAS annual AI Literacy session each year.
  • AI must not be used to generate evaluative comments about individual students without substantial human review and rewriting.
  • Staff are encouraged to share AI tools that work well in the classroom through the school's internal AI working group.

9. Policy Review

This policy will be reviewed annually, no later than February each year. The review process will include a survey of parents, students (Grades 6–12), and staff. Feedback from all three groups will be considered before any changes are adopted. The next scheduled review is February 2027.

The AI landscape changes quickly. IAS reserves the right to update this policy at any time in response to significant changes in technology, law, or IB/Cognia guidance. Parents and staff will be notified of any material changes within 10 school days.
